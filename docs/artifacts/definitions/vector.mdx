<Purpose>

Attack vectors and techniques used to compromise information systems.

</Purpose>

<Role>

Vectors name the pathways through which harm can arrive. In a contribution
programme they are not abstract MITRE IDs alone—they are concrete ways an
employee contribution can go wrong: an accidental sensitive commit, an
unreviewed public release, or contribution under the wrong identity. Guidance
cites them; threats treat them as attack paths.

</Role>

<Examples>

<RevealItem title="Accidental commit of sensitive information" defaultOpen>

A contributor inadvertently includes credentials, client data or internal
hostnames in a public commit or issue. The vector names the pathway; later
threats and controls reason about disclosure risk along that path.

**Source:** [Accidental Commit of Sensitive Information](https://github.com/robmoffat/FOSS-Contribution-Gemara/blob/main/vector.yaml) (`accidental-sensitive-commit`) in *FOSS Contribution Vector Catalog*.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Unreviewed public release">

Material reaches a public repository before information-classification,
export-control or IP review completes. The pathway is premature publication—not
the control that would have caught it.

**Source:** [Unreviewed Public Release](https://github.com/robmoffat/FOSS-Contribution-Gemara/blob/main/vector.yaml) (`unreviewed-public-release`) in *FOSS Contribution Vector Catalog*.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Improper company-identity attribution">

Contribution is performed under a company-associated identity when the activity
should have been personal—or the reverse—creating a pathway for misattributed
legal and reputational exposure.

**Source:** [Company Identity Misuse](https://github.com/robmoffat/FOSS-Contribution-Gemara/blob/main/vector.yaml) (`company-identity-misuse`) in *FOSS Contribution Vector Catalog*.

</RevealItem>

</Examples>

<AntiPatterns>

<RevealItem title="Vectors that are really threats">

“Data leakage” is an organisational threat or risk. “Accidental sensitive
commit” is a vector—the pathway that enables the threat.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Generic vectors with no applicability">

A catalogue of techniques that never say which contribution categories or
systems they apply to. Pathways without scope cannot inform controls.

</RevealItem>

</AntiPatterns>
