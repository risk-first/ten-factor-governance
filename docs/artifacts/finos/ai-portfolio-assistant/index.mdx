# AI Portfolio Assistant

Consider a financial institution providing portfolio managers with an **AI Portfolio Assistant** operating across a suite of FDC3 applications. The assistant can access the user's portfolio, invoke specialist applications to calculate exposures and stress scenarios, retrieve relevant investment research, and explain the results. Rather than asking the AI to perform financial calculations itself, deterministic applications provide portfolio, risk and market information, while the AI combines and interprets those results to help the portfolio manager understand their positions and risks.

As the assistant becomes more capable, however, the consequences of its actions increase. Reading a portfolio or explaining a risk may require relatively little authority, while recommending that the portfolio manager reduce a position is more consequential. Constructing a proposed set of orders goes further still, and actually submitting those orders would give the AI direct authority over the portfolio. Governance therefore needs to control **how much authority is delegated to the AI and under what conditions**. An organisation might allow the assistant to analyse and explain autonomously, permit recommendations only when specified controls have been satisfied, require human approval for proposed orders, and prohibit autonomous trade execution entirely.

This example follows that progression from **observe → analyse → explain → recommend → propose → execute**. It demonstrates how Ten-Factor Governance can make the boundaries around AI authority explicit, machine-readable, enforceable and auditable across a real multi-application workflow. The example also shows how those governance mechanisms can form part of an ISO/IEC 42001-aligned AI management system: establishing responsibility for the AI system, identifying and treating its risks and impacts, controlling its operation, retaining appropriate human oversight, collecting evidence about its behaviour, and using that evidence to evaluate and improve its governance over time.
