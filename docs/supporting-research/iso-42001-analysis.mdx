# ISO/IEC 42001 Analysis

ISO/IEC 42001 is an organisation-wide Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS) standard. [Ten Factor Governance](/docs/factors/) is an architectural model for making governance operable.

They are complementary rather than interchangeable: Ten Factor aligns strongly with ISO planning, operation, evaluation and evidence, while the outer management-system envelope (context, leadership, objectives, resources, competence, management review, CAPA) and AI-specific objects (impact assessment, AI system/data/supplier schemas) remain the largest gaps.

**Coverage labels:** 
 - **Full** = direct corresponding capability (not by itself ISO conformity); 
 - **Partial** = useful machinery exists but an ISO-required element is missing; 
 - **Does not cover** / **Not represented** = no material direct representation.

Requirement descriptions below are concise paraphrases of public ISO material, not a substitute for a licensed copy of the standard.

## Clauses and management-system requirements

<Comparison result="Partial" item="4.1" title="Understanding the organisation and its context">

<Requirement>

Determine internal and external issues relevant to the organisation's purpose and its ability to achieve the intended outcomes of the AIMS.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Build a Governance Twin](/docs/factors/build-a-governance-twin) gives each sensitive activity a machine-readable governance representation
- [Context In, Decisions Out](/docs/factors/context-in-decisions-out) makes runtime context explicit

</AddressedBy>

Neither is an organisation-level context assessment.

<Gap>

Context can be consumed, but the ISO management exercise establishing internal/external context is not recorded as a first-class artefact.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add a generic **OrganisationContext** artefact/profile covering internal/external issues, jurisdictions, organisational boundaries and change history.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial" item="4.2" title="Needs and expectations of interested parties">

<Requirement>

Identify interested parties relevant to the AIMS and determine which of their requirements are relevant to the management system.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Governance Is Composable](/docs/factors/governance-is-composable) discusses regulator, customer and organisational overlays
- [Governance Has Owners](/docs/factors/governance-has-owners) identifies accountable organisations

</AddressedBy>

Interested-party requirements themselves are not represented.

<Gap>

Stakeholders can appear around artefacts, but stakeholder requirements are not a distinct object.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add **InterestedParty** and **StakeholderRequirement** schemas with mappings into [Guidance](/docs/artifacts/definitions/guidance), [Control](/docs/artifacts/definitions/control), [Risk](/docs/artifacts/definitions/risk) and [Policy](/docs/artifacts/definitions/policy).

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial" item="4.3" title="Determining the scope of the AIMS">

<Requirement>

Define the boundaries and applicability of the AIMS, considering organisational context, interested-party requirements and interfaces/dependencies, and maintain the scope as documented information.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Govern Sensitive Activities](/docs/factors/govern-sensitive-activities) provides a strong operational boundary
- [Govern Dependencies](/docs/factors/govern-dependencies) extends governance through dependency relationships

</AddressedBy>

No formal AIMS scope exists.

<Gap>

Strong operational scoping, weak management-system scoping.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add an **AIMSScope/GovernanceScope** profile referencing organisations, systems, activities, sites, suppliers and exclusions.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial, but architecturally strong" item="4.4" title="AI management system">

<Requirement>

Establish, implement, maintain and continually improve the AIMS and the processes/interactions needed for it.

</Requirement>

The complete [Ten Factors](/docs/factors/) provide a blueprint for operable governance, especially twins, versioning, evidence and continuous governance.

<Gap>

The factors provide much of an implementation substrate, but not an explicit AIMS object/process model.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Publish an **ISO 42001 profile for Ten Factor** rather than changing the core model.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial" item="5.1" title="Leadership and commitment">

<Requirement>

Top management must demonstrate leadership, integrate AIMS requirements into business processes, provide resources, support relevant roles and promote continual improvement.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Governance Has Owners](/docs/factors/governance-has-owners) strongly requires accountability and authority to change governance, approve exceptions and respond to failure

</AddressedBy>

It does not require top-management sponsorship or strategic integration.

<Gap>

Accountability is not the same as top-management commitment.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add a **GovernanceAuthority/ManagementCommitment** artefact or AIMS profile identifying executive sponsor, authority, resources and approvals.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial–strong" item="5.2" title="AI policy">

<Requirement>

Establish an AI policy appropriate to the organisation's purpose and strategic direction, providing a framework for objectives and commitments, and communicate/maintain it.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Build a Governance Twin](/docs/factors/build-a-governance-twin) explicitly includes policy
- [Governance is Version Controlled](/docs/factors/governance-is-version-controlled) requires reviewed, approved and versioned changes
- [Governance Is Composable](/docs/factors/governance-is-composable) supports enterprise and local policy layers

</AddressedBy>

<Gap>

Excellent operational policy machinery; incomplete ISO organisational-policy semantics.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Define an **ISO42001 AI Policy profile** with required purpose, commitments, strategic alignment, approver and review cadence.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial–strong" item="5.3" title="Organisational roles, responsibilities and authorities">

<Requirement>

Assign and communicate responsibilities and authorities needed for the AIMS, including responsibility for ensuring conformity and reporting AIMS performance to top management.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Governance Has Owners](/docs/factors/governance-has-owners) says every Twin, artefact and decision needs an accountable owner

</AddressedBy>

It does not define the complete AIMS responsibility structure or reporting chain.

<Gap>

Strong object-level accountability, incomplete management-system responsibility model.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add reusable **GovernanceRole / Authority / Escalation** entities referenced by all governance artefacts.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial" item="6.1.1" title="Actions addressing risks and opportunities: general">

<Requirement>

Plan actions for AI-related risks and opportunities so the AIMS can achieve intended results, prevent undesired effects and improve.

</Requirement>

[Govern Sensitive Activities](/docs/factors/govern-sensitive-activities) starts from unacceptable risk; twins combine risks/controls/policy and [Continuous Governance](/docs/factors/continuous-governance) feeds operational learning back. Opportunities are not first-class.

<Gap>

Notably stronger on downside risk than on positive opportunity management.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Extend the management-system profile with **Opportunity** or generalise Risk into an **Effect of Uncertainty** model where needed.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial–strong" item="6.1.2" title="AI risk assessment">

<Requirement>

Define and apply a repeatable AI risk-assessment process, identifying risks, analysing likelihood/consequences and evaluating them against risk criteria.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Build a Governance Twin](/docs/factors/build-a-governance-twin) makes risk part of the activity's governance
- [Govern Dependencies](/docs/factors/govern-dependencies) adds inherited supply-chain risk

</AddressedBy>

The method of risk scoring is deliberately not prescribed.

<Gap>

Good representation, but no complete ISO-specific risk-assessment procedure/methodology.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Define an **ISO42001 Risk Assessment profile** with assessment context, likelihood, consequence, criteria, date, assessor and review trigger.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial–strong" item="6.1.3" title="AI risk treatment">

<Requirement>

Select treatment options and controls, determine necessary controls, compare them with Annex A, produce a treatment plan and record applicability, including accepted residual risk. The resulting controls are reflected in the Statement of Applicability.

</Requirement>

Twins link risks, controls and policy; [Governance Is Composable](/docs/factors/governance-is-composable) supports importing selected shared controls; [Governance is Version Controlled](/docs/factors/governance-is-version-controlled) supports explicit exceptions. There is no Statement of Applicability artefact.

<Gap>

The underlying machinery largely exists, but ISO's explicit applicability decision record does not.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add **ApplicabilityProfile / StatementOfApplicability** mapping each Annex A control to applicable/not-applicable, rationale, implementation state, risk and evidence.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial but materially incomplete" item="6.1.4" title="AI system impact assessment">

<Requirement>

Establish a process for assessing and documenting potential consequences of AI systems on individuals, groups and society, feeding the results into AI risk assessment and treatment.

</Requirement>

Sensitive activities can include safety/legal/business risks and twins can contain risks, but there is no dedicated impact-assessment model for affected people/groups/society.

<Gap>

Major AI-specific gap.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add **ImpactAssessment** and **Impact** schemas mapped to AI system/activity, affected parties, intended/foreseeable use, risks, controls and assessment evidence.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Does not cover directly" item="6.2" title="AI objectives and planning to achieve them">

<Requirement>

Establish measurable AI objectives aligned with the AI policy; determine actions, resources, responsibilities, timelines and how results will be evaluated, and retain documented information.

</Requirement>

Controls and governance decisions have objectives implicitly, and owners/continuous governance support delivery, but organisational AIMS objectives and KPIs are absent.

<Gap>

No first-class management objectives/metrics model.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add **ManagementObjective / Metric / Target / MeasurementPeriod** artefacts linked to policy, owners and evaluation results.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial–strong" item="6.3" title="Planning of changes">

<Requirement>

Changes to the AIMS should be carried out in a planned manner, considering purpose, consequences, integrity, resources and responsibilities.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Governance is Version Controlled](/docs/factors/governance-is-version-controlled) requires review, testing, approval and promotion of governance changes
- [Continuous Governance](/docs/factors/continuous-governance) adds lifecycle feedback

</AddressedBy>

<Gap>

TFG is particularly well aligned to controlled governance change; ISO's management-level change planning is broader.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add a **GovernanceChange** record referencing changed artefacts, rationale, impact, owner, approvals, resources and rollout.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial" item="7.1" title="Resources">

<Requirement>

Determine and provide resources needed to establish, implement, maintain and continually improve the AIMS.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Govern Dependencies](/docs/factors/govern-dependencies) treats software, data, services, infrastructure and organisations as dependencies
- [Governance Has Owners](/docs/factors/governance-has-owners) notes owners need authority and resources

</AddressedBy>

It is not an AIMS resource-planning process.

<Gap>

Resources are dependencies/targets, not management resources.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add a **GovernanceResource** schema/profile covering human, data, tooling, compute, financial and external resources.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Does not cover directly" item="7.2" title="Competence">

<Requirement>

Determine competence required for people affecting AIMS performance, ensure competence through education/training/experience and retain evidence.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Governance Has Owners](/docs/factors/governance-has-owners) requires owners capable of doing the work, but does not define competence requirements, training or competence evidence

</AddressedBy>

<Gap>

Competence appears only as evidence of something else, not as a first-class concept.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add **CompetenceRequirement**, **TrainingRecord** and mappings to roles/activities.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Does not cover" item="7.3" title="Awareness">

<Requirement>

Relevant personnel should understand the AI policy, their contribution to AIMS effectiveness and implications of nonconformity.

</Requirement>

Ownership creates accountability but not workforce awareness.

<Gap>

No awareness/attestation mechanism.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add an **AwarenessRequirement/Attestation** mechanism to a management-system profile rather than a new core factor.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial/weak" item="7.4" title="Communication">

<Requirement>

Determine what AIMS-related communications are required, when they occur, with whom and by what process.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Governance Has Owners](/docs/factors/governance-has-owners) supports escalation and incident response, and [Evidence by Default](/docs/factors/evidence-by-default) can be reused for reporting, but no communication plan exists

</AddressedBy>

<Gap>

Exception/noncompliance communication is better represented than general AIMS communication.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add **CommunicationRequirement** with subject, audience, trigger, channel, owner and evidence of communication.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial–strong" item="7.5" title="Documented information">

<Requirement>

Create, update and control documented information needed by the standard and the organisation, including identification, review/approval, access, storage, protection, versioning, retention and disposition.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Governance is Version Controlled](/docs/factors/governance-is-version-controlled) requires governance to be versioned, reviewed, tested and promoted with a history of change

</AddressedBy>

It is weaker on generic records management such as retention/disposition/access protection.

<Gap>

Excellent “governance as code”; incomplete records-management semantics.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Extend common metadata with **approval, effective date, supersession, retention, disposition and access-classification** fields where appropriate.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial–strong / closest alignment" item="8.1" title="Operational planning and control">

<Requirement>

Plan, implement and control processes needed to meet AIMS requirements and implement Clause 6 actions; establish criteria, operate to those criteria and manage planned/unintended changes and externally provided processes.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Govern Sensitive Activities](/docs/factors/govern-sensitive-activities), [Build a Governance Twin](/docs/factors/build-a-governance-twin), [Context In, Decisions Out](/docs/factors/context-in-decisions-out), [Continuous Governance](/docs/factors/continuous-governance) and [Govern Dependencies](/docs/factors/govern-dependencies) together establish governed activities, definitions, decision boundaries, continuous verification and external dependencies

</AddressedBy>

<Gap>

Ten Factor operationalises this better than most document-centric frameworks, but does not by itself define the complete AIMS process estate.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Document a normative **Governed Activity lifecycle** profile showing ISO planning criteria → Twin → Policy/Controls → Evaluation/Enforcement.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial" item="8.2" title="AI risk assessment in operation">

<Requirement>

Perform AI risk assessments at planned intervals and when significant changes are proposed or occur, retaining results.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Continuous Governance](/docs/factors/continuous-governance) and [Govern Dependencies](/docs/factors/govern-dependencies) provide excellent triggers for reassessment when implementation, data/model or dependencies change

</AddressedBy>

<Gap>

Missing a first-class time-stamped RiskAssessment record tying methodology, inputs and conclusions together.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add **RiskAssessmentLog** or enrich Risk with assessment instances/history.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial–strong" item="8.3" title="AI risk treatment in operation">

<Requirement>

Implement the risk-treatment plan and retain documented results.

</Requirement>

Twin policy/controls, [Context In, Decisions Out](/docs/factors/context-in-decisions-out), [Continuous Governance](/docs/factors/continuous-governance) and [Evidence by Default](/docs/factors/evidence-by-default) operationalise treatment rather than leaving it in a plan.

<Gap>

Strong execution chain; weaker formal treatment-plan/status artefact.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add treatment status, owner, due date and residual-risk decision to the proposed **Applicability/RiskTreatment** profile.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial/weak" item="8.4" title="AI system impact assessment in operation">

<Requirement>

Perform AI-system impact assessments at planned intervals and when relevant changes occur, and retain the results.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Continuous Governance](/docs/factors/continuous-governance) provides change triggers, but impact assessment itself is not represented

</AddressedBy>

<Gap>

<Comparison result="Partial–strong" item="9.1" title="Monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation">

<Requirement>

Determine what should be monitored/measured, methods and timing, analyse results and evaluate AIMS performance/effectiveness, retaining evidence.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Evidence by Default](/docs/factors/evidence-by-default) makes evidence an ordinary output
- [Continuous Governance](/docs/factors/continuous-governance) continuously tests and observes behaviour/drift

</AddressedBy>

Organisational KPIs are not first-class.

<Gap>

Excellent control-level observability; incomplete AIMS objective/KPI performance model.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add **Metric/ObjectiveResult** alongside Evaluation Log and allow ManagementReview to consume it.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial" item="9.2" title="Internal audit">

<Requirement>

Conduct internal audits at planned intervals to determine whether the AIMS conforms to organisational and ISO requirements and is effectively implemented; maintain an audit programme, impartiality and results.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Governance Measures](/docs/artifacts/measures/) includes audit reviewing evaluation/enforcement evidence
- [Evidence by Default](/docs/factors/evidence-by-default) and [Governance Has Owners](/docs/factors/governance-has-owners) support auditability

</AddressedBy>

An audit programme/independence model is not supplied.

<Gap>

Audit results are well represented; audit governance is not.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add **AuditProgramme** with scope, cadence, auditor independence/competence, schedule and follow-up.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial but not equivalent" item="9.3" title="Management review">

<Requirement>

Top management reviews the AIMS at planned intervals for continuing suitability, adequacy and effectiveness, considering changes, performance, audits, nonconformities and prior actions; decisions/actions are recorded.

</Requirement>

[Governance Has Owners](/docs/factors/governance-has-owners) says owners review evidence, investigate failures and publish improvements, which resembles local governance review; formal top-management AIMS review is not represented.

<Gap>

One of the largest management-system gaps.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add **ManagementReview** with period, attendees, context changes, objectives/KPIs, audit/NCR/risk inputs, decisions, resources and improvement actions.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial–strong" item="10.1" title="Continual improvement">

<Requirement>

Continually improve the suitability, adequacy and effectiveness of the AIMS.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Continuous Governance](/docs/factors/continuous-governance) creates an engineering feedback loop
- [Governance Has Owners](/docs/factors/governance-has-owners) explicitly says owners turn evidence and incidents into improved governance

</AddressedBy>

<Gap>

TFG captures the engineering spirit very well, but not the formal AIMS-wide process.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add **ImprovementAction** linked from review/audit/evaluation back to risk, policy, control and objective revisions.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

<Comparison result="Partial" item="10.2" title="Nonconformity and corrective action">

<Requirement>

React to nonconformity, control/correct consequences, investigate causes and possible recurrence elsewhere, implement corrective action, verify effectiveness, update the AIMS if necessary and retain evidence.

</Requirement>

<AddressedBy>

- [Continuous Governance](/docs/factors/continuous-governance) identifies failures
- [Governance Has Owners](/docs/factors/governance-has-owners) investigate and improve governance
- [Evidence by Default](/docs/factors/evidence-by-default) records what happened

</AddressedBy>

Root-cause/CAPA lifecycle and effectiveness review are not first-class.

<Gap>

“Enforcement” is narrower than ISO corrective action.

</Gap>

<Recommendation>

Add **Nonconformity/CAPA** with containment, root cause, systemic scope, corrective action, owner, deadline, effectiveness test and closure.

</Recommendation>

</Comparison>

## Synthesis

Across Annex A, **Ten Factor often has the right architectural principle but not the domain object**, and it lacks the AI-specific schemas ISO expects. Deployment, verification/validation, operation/monitoring, event evidence, responsible use and supplier governance fit existing factors without inventing new principles—the missing work is mostly concrete interoperable artefacts and profiles.

The highest-value additions remain **ImpactAssessment** (largest AI-specific semantic hole) and **ManagementReview/CAPA** (largest management-system structural hole). The intended division of labour:

- **ISO/IEC 42001** — what organisational management system must exist for responsible AI
- **[Ten Factor Governance](/docs/factors/)** — what architectural properties make that governance operable
