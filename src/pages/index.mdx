Modern software increasingly operates in environments where trust,
accountability and compliance are not optional—they are fundamental
requirements. Whether building systems for financial services, healthcare,
critical infrastructure or government, organisations are expected to
demonstrate not only that their software works, but that it is secure,
resilient, auditable and governed throughout its lifecycle.

Ten Factor Governance provides a practical architectural blueprint for
building systems that can meet these expectations by design.

<aside className={styles.revealAside} aria-labelledby="why-now-heading">
<h2 id="why-now-heading" className={styles.revealAsideTitle}>
  Why Now?
</h2>

<RevealItem title="Software is making increasingly important decisions." defaultOpen>

Applications, automation and **AI agents** now approve payments, deploy
infrastructure, access sensitive data and influence business outcomes with
little or no human intervention.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Regulation is shifting from documentation to demonstrable governance.">

Frameworks such as EU DORA, the EU AI Act, NIS2 and industry-specific regulations
increasingly expect continuous evidence, traceability and accountability—not
annual compliance exercises.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Software supply chains have become part of the attack surface.">

Modern applications depend on open source, cloud services, AI models and
automated build pipelines. Governing the runtime alone is no longer sufficient.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Governance remains fragmented.">

Policies live in documents, controls in spreadsheets, inventories in CMDBs,
evidence in CI/CD systems and decisions inside application code. Organisations
struggle to answer simple questions about what is governed and why.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Machine-readable governance is finally possible.">

Open standards for policy, provenance, observability, identity and governance—and
the ability of LLMs to author, explain and operate over those artefacts—now make
it practical to treat governance as an architectural concern rather than a
manual process.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Governance must become part of software architecture.">

Just as [The Twelve-Factor App](https://12factor.net/) (Adam Wiggins, 2011)
defined principles for building cloud-native software, modern systems need
architectural principles for making governance continuous, observable and
automatable.

</RevealItem>
